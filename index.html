Indiclock — molecular aging layer
Frozen v2.1: 275 CpGs, trained on whole-blood 450K data. In the Indian-classified GSE55763/LOLIPOP validation subset, the frozen model achieved Pearson R 0.941, MAE 3.50 years and RMSE 4.37 years without retraining or recalibration. This demonstrates transferability, not completed India-specific optimization or clinical validation.
IndiBio — physiological aging layer
Frozen v2-37: 38-biomarker architecture across seven physiological domains. It was developed using NHANES and temporally tested in NHANES 2003–04. The external result (R 0.769, MAE 8.79 years) makes population transportability and calibration a central development question.
Biological State Engine — the integration layer
The next research step is paired analysis: molecular age ↔ physiological age, CpG ↔ physiological-domain relationships and, where sample size permits, CpG ↔ individual biomarkers. The goal is to learn whether the two layers overlap, complement each other or diverge.
BioStack — future intervention layer
A future product direction: translate measured biological state into evidence-based, personalized actions and repeat measurements over time. This is a product direction, not a clinically validated intervention platform today.

Indian transportability programme
Large-scale Indian laboratory/report-level evidence from NidaanKosha has identified biomarker-specific distribution and age-trajectory signals across multiple physiological domains. It is not treated as a 100,000-person cohort. No IndiBio coefficient has been changed on this evidence alone. Independent Indian participant-level replication remains the next filter.
What is being tested next
Priority: GenomeIndia phenotype-data audit, participant-level Indian replication, and paired cohorts containing both methylation and relevant blood biomarkers. High-priority trajectory candidates currently include albumin, total protein, ALT and AST, with replication required before any model recalibration.
