The product

Three layers that turn biological data into an interpretable state.

Aeterna is not designed as a single “biological age calculator.” The product architecture starts with two independently measurable biological layers, then connects them through a mapping layer. The long-term output is an interpretable, longitudinal biological state that can reveal where molecular and physiological aging agree, diverge, or change over time.

01 · IndiBio — physiological layer

What it measures: systemic physiological state using routine blood biomarkers across seven domains: metabolic, inflammation, cardiovascular, renal, hematologic/iron, hepatic/protein and nutritional.

Why it matters: these are accessible measurements already used in routine healthcare. IndiBio is intended to turn many individual laboratory values into a structured picture of physiological aging rather than interpreting each marker in isolation.

02 · Indiclock — molecular layer

What it measures: molecular aging through DNA methylation in whole blood. The frozen v2.1 model uses 275 CpG features selected from a larger age-associated feature space.

Why it matters: DNA methylation provides a different biological layer from routine chemistry and hematology. It gives Aeterna a molecular readout that can be studied alongside physiological state.

03 · Mapping layer

What it does: connects molecular and physiological measurements in the same participants. The planned analysis includes molecular-age ↔ physiological-age association, CpG ↔ physiological-domain relationships, and—where sample size supports it—individual CpG ↔ biomarker associations.

Why it matters: the mapping layer tests whether the two measurements capture overlapping biology, complementary biology, or specific relationships that can make the final system more interpretable.

From measurements to the final product

Input: methylation data + routine blood biomarkers + age/sex/context, where available.

Layer 1: IndiBio organizes routine biomarkers into physiological domains and estimates physiological state.

Layer 2: Indiclock converts methylation patterns into a molecular-aging estimate.

Mapping: the system studies how these layers relate within the same person and across time.

Future Biological State Engine: integrates molecular state, physiological state, context, trajectories and discordance into an interpretable longitudinal profile. This is the product direction; the integrated mapping is an active research programme, not yet a clinically validated product.

Why population-aware biology?

Why start with India — and why expand to South Asia?

Aeterna starts in India because population-specific biological measurement is most useful when it is anchored in a clearly defined population, real-world data and a large unmet preventive-health need. India is the first biological reference context—not the assumption that one Indian profile represents every person.

1 · Biology is not only about body weight

Research in Asian Indian and broader South Asian populations has repeatedly described higher body-fat and central/visceral-fat burden at lower BMI levels than is typical of white European populations. This can create metabolic risk before conventional BMI-based categories make the risk obvious. In practical terms, the same BMI or laboratory value can carry different biological context across populations.

2 · Metabolic risk can appear at lower adiposity

South Asian populations have been reported to show greater insulin resistance, dysglycaemia, atherogenic dyslipidaemia and cardiometabolic risk at comparatively lower levels of adiposity. That does not mean every South Asian individual has the same physiology; it means population-level reference models deserve to be tested rather than assumed to transfer unchanged.

3 · India is the right starting reference population

India combines enormous population scale with substantial regional, socioeconomic, dietary and environmental diversity and a major burden of cardiometabolic disease. Building the evidence base here allows Aeterna to test whether molecular and physiological measurements remain well calibrated in the population it intends to serve.

Why India first, then South Asia?

India → South Asia is a validation strategy, not a shortcut. India gives Aeterna a concrete starting population in which to establish datasets, reference distributions and biological trajectories. The next question is whether the observed relationships generalize across neighbouring South Asian populations. Some features may be shared; others may vary by ancestry, geography, diet, environment, healthcare access, age structure, sex distribution and disease burden.

That is why Aeterna's goal is not to create an “Indian-only” black box. The long-term goal is a population-aware reference framework that can identify when a model transfers, when calibration is required, and when a population-specific model is scientifically justified.

What this means for Aeterna

Population specificity enters at three levels: reference distributions (what is typical), age trajectories (how biomarkers change with age), and eventually model effects (whether the relationship between a biological signal and the target changes enough to justify recalibration). Aeterna's current Indian programme is deliberately testing these levels in sequence rather than assuming them.

External evidence supports population differences in adiposity and cardiometabolic risk, including lower BMI thresholds for risk classification in Asian populations. These findings motivate transportability testing; they do not establish that all Indians or South Asians have a single distinct biological phenotype.

Selected evidence

Nature Reviews Endocrinology (2026) discusses excess visceral/ectopic adiposity, reduced beta-cell reserve and heightened cardiometabolic risk at lower BMI thresholds in Asian populations. Asian Indian BMI evidence documents higher body fat and metabolic perturbations at lower BMI values. Asian Indian consensus evidence describes excess abdominal and ectopic fat and lower BMI thresholds for obesity classification.

Building evidence before making claims.

I deliberately separate frozen technology, external validation, transportability analysis and future calibration. The goal is to know what the data support before changing a model or making a clinical claim.

What are 275 CpGs?

CpGs are specific DNA sites where methylation can be measured. The model does not say that all 275 sites individually “cause aging.” They are the molecular features that, together, carried predictive information for age in the development data and were retained by the frozen model.

What does R = 0.941 mean?

Pearson R describes how strongly predicted age tracks chronological age across the validation samples. A value close to 1 means the two measurements move closely together; it does not mean the model is 94.1% accurate.

What does MAE = 3.50 years mean?

Mean absolute error is the average size of the prediction error, ignoring whether the model was above or below the true age. Here, the average absolute difference was about 3.5 years in the frozen external validation subset.

And RMSE = 4.37 years?

RMSE measures prediction error too, but it penalizes larger errors more strongly than MAE. An RMSE of 4.37 years alongside a 3.50-year MAE indicates that most errors are smaller than the largest errors, while making those larger deviations visible. Together, R, MAE and RMSE give an investor a more useful picture than any one metric alone.

Applied without retraining to the Indian-classified GSE55763/LOLIPOP subset. This demonstrates strong external transferability; it is not presented as proof that the model has already been optimized for Indian biology.

Why routine biomarkers?

Blood chemistry and hematology are comparatively accessible and already embedded in clinical workflows. IndiBio is designed to interpret them as a coordinated physiological ageing system rather than as disconnected lab values.

Why seven domains?

The domain structure preserves biological meaning: metabolic, inflammatory, cardiovascular, renal, hematologic/iron, hepatic/protein and nutritional signals can be examined separately before being integrated.

Why test India separately?

A reference model built in one population may not transfer perfectly to another. The current Indian programme therefore measures transportability first and changes the frozen model only if independent evidence supports it.

The frozen NHANES-derived model is being tested for transportability to Indian populations rather than silently recalibrated. Its current evidence is temporal external validation, not completed Indian clinical validation.

Mapping layer — the bridge between the two measurements

The central scientific question is not simply “which layer is better?” It is whether molecular and physiological aging provide the same signal, different but complementary signal, or measurable relationships that can improve interpretation.

The planned paired analysis uses participants with methylation + blood biomarkers + age/sex/context where available. It will test: molecular age ↔ physiological age; 275 CpGs ↔ seven physiological domains; and, where sample size permits, CpGs ↔ individual biomarkers. Routine blood biomarkers may also be tested as an exploratory explanation of part of the molecular-aging signal, but that relationship is not assumed in advance.

A high overlap would support exploration of blood-based surrogacy; low overlap could show that the layers are complementary. The analysis is designed to measure this rather than force the two layers into one score.
